## The six moves
1. **Sketch and name the system.** Draw the situation. Circle the one object you will write the law for.
2. **Draw the free-body diagram.** One arrow per force ON that object. Label each as "force by what, on what". Equal forces, equal lengths.
3. **Choose the positive direction.** Pick the way the object accelerates, or the way it would. Forces that point that way are positive.
4. **Write the sum of forces.** ΣF = ma with every term signed. Write it in symbols before any number goes in.
5. **Solve with units.** Isolate the unknown, substitute, carry units. N is kg·m/s².
6. **Sense-check.** Direction right? Size believable? Does it pass a limit (zero mass, zero push)?

## Model problems

### M1 (I do): A 2.0 kg book rests on a table
Find the normal force on the book.

Predict: Before any math: is the table pushing harder than, equal to, or less than the pull of gravity? Why? → Equal. The book is not accelerating, so the arrows cancel.

1. System: the book. Interactions: the book with Earth (field) and the book with the table (contact).
2. FBD: W down (Earth on book), N up (table on book). Same length.
3. Positive: up.
4. ΣF = N − W = ma, and a = 0.
5. N = W = mg = (2.0 kg)(9.8 m/s²) = 19.6 N.
6. Check: up, same size as the weight. Pass. Note N equals W because a = 0, not because they are a third-law pair.

Common slip: Calling N and W an action-reaction pair. They act on the same object, so they cannot be.

### M2 (I do): A 12 kg crate is pushed right with 80 N; friction is 50 N
Find its acceleration.

Predict: Does it move at constant speed, speed up, or slow down? Which way does the net force point? → Speeds up to the right. The push beats friction, so the net force points right.

1. System: the crate.
2. FBD: F = 80 N right, f = 50 N left, W and N cancel and are equal and opposite.
3. Positive: right.
4. ΣF = F − f = ma. Up and down add to zero.
5. a = (80 − 50) N / 12 kg = 2.5 m/s².
6. Check: positive means right, matches the prediction. 30 N on 12 kg is a bit over 2. Pass.

Common slip: Adding friction to the push. Friction points against the slide, so its sign is negative.

### M3 (we do): A 60 kg student stands on a scale in an elevator that speeds up upward at 1.5 m/s²
What does the scale read?

Predict: Heavier than, equal to, or lighter than the 588 N at rest? Why? → Heavier. To accelerate upward the floor must push up more than gravity pulls down.

1. System: the student, not the elevator.
2. FBD: N up (scale on student), W down. N is longer than W.
3. Positive: up.
4. ΣF = N − mg = ma.
5. N = m(g + a) = 60(9.8 + 1.5) = 678 N.
6. Check: bigger than 588 N. If a = 0 the formula gives mg. Pass.

Common slip: Thinking the scale reads weight. A scale reads the normal force on you.

### M4 (we do): A 4.0 kg block on a frictionless table is tied to a 2.0 kg hanging block over a light pulley
Find the acceleration and the string tension.

Predict: Is the tension more than, equal to, or less than the 19.6 N weight of the hanging block? → Less. The hanging block speeds up downward, so gravity beats the string.

1. Two objects, so two diagrams. Block 1: T right (string), W and N cancel. Block 2: T up, W2 down.
2. Same string, same T. Same speed, same a.
3. Positive: the direction of motion, right for block 1 and down for block 2.
4. Block 1: T = m1 a. Block 2: m2 g − T = m2 a.
5. Add: m2 g = (m1 + m2) a, so a = 19.6/6.0 = 3.27 m/s². Then T = m1 a = 13.1 N.
6. Check: T < 19.6 N. If m1 is huge, a → 0. If m1 → 0, a → g. Pass.

Common slip: Setting T equal to the hanging weight. That is only true if the block is not accelerating.

### M5 (you do): Two blocks touch on a frictionless floor: 3.0 kg and 5.0 kg. A 24 N push acts on the 3.0 kg block
Find the force the 3.0 kg block exerts on the 5.0 kg block.

Predict: More than 24 N, equal, or less? Which block feels more net force? → Less than 24 N. The 5.0 kg block gets only part of the push.

1. Draw the system of both blocks first: only the 24 N is external. a = 24/8.0 = 3.0 m/s².
2. Now the 5.0 kg block alone: the contact force C is the only horizontal force.
3. Positive: right.
4. C = m2 a.
5. C = 5.0 × 3.0 = 15 N.
6. Check: 15 < 24. By the third law the 5.0 kg block pushes back on the 3.0 kg block with 15 N, leaving it 9 N: 9/3.0 = 3.0. Pass.

Common slip: Saying the 5.0 kg block feels the full 24 N.

## Ladder (one sample per task type)

### Band 1: Core

- **A rope pulls a sled. Which two objects interact, and is the force contact or field?**
  - Answer: rope and sled, contact
  - Worked: Every force has two objects: one pushes or pulls, one is pushed or pulled. Touching means contact. Acting across a gap means field.
  - Misconception: A force belongs to one object; a force never comes from "motion".
- **A 4.0 kg bag hangs motionless from a rope. How big is the rope tension?**
  - Answer: 39.2 N
  - Worked: At rest, the arrows cancel: T = W. W = mg = 4 × 9.8 = 39.2 N.
  - Misconception: Students think the rope pulls harder than gravity because it "holds" the bag.
- **A crate gets a 45 N push to the right and 5 N of friction to the left. Net force, right positive?**
  - Answer: 40 N
  - Worked: Right is positive, so friction is −5. 45 − 5 = 40 N, to the right.
  - Misconception: Adding forces that point opposite ways.
- **A sled slides at constant speed while you pull with 28 N. How big is the friction on it?**
  - Answer: 28 N
  - Worked: Constant speed means no acceleration, so the net force is zero. Friction equals the pull: 28 N.
  - Misconception: Needing a bigger forward force to keep moving at constant speed.
### Band 2: Honors

- **A 6 kg crate gets a 20 N push right and 10 N of friction left. Take right as positive. Find the acceleration.**
  - Answer: 1.67 m/s²
  - Worked: ΣF = 20 − 10 = 10 N. a = ΣF/m = 10/6 = 1.67 m/s².
  - Misconception: Dividing the push alone by the mass.
- **A rope pulls a 7 kg bucket straight up with 75.6 N. Take up as positive. Find the acceleration.**
  - Answer: 1 m/s²
  - Worked: W = 68.6 N down. ΣF = T − W = 7 N. a = 1 m/s² upward.
  - Misconception: Forgetting weight when a rope pulls.
- **A 75 kg person stands on a scale in an elevator that rises at constant speed. What does the scale read?**
  - Answer: 735 N
  - Worked: Constant speed: a = 0, so N = W. N = mg = 735 N.
  - Misconception: Believing motion upward makes the scale read more.
- **A net force of 14 N gives an object an acceleration of 2 m/s². What is its mass?**
  - Answer: 7 kg
  - Worked: m = ΣF/a = 14/2 = 7 kg.
  - Misconception: Multiplying instead of dividing.
### Band 3: AP Physics 1

- **A 5 kg block on a table (μk = 0.25) is tied over a light frictionless pulley to a 3 kg hanging block. Find the acceleration.**
  - Symbolic: a = (m2 − μ m1) g / (m1 + m2)
  - Answer: 2.14 m/s²
  - Worked: Block 1: T − μ m1 g = m1 a. Block 2: m2 g − T = m2 a. Add: m2 g − μ m1 g = (m1 + m2) a. a = 2.14 m/s².
  - Misconception: Using m2 g as the only net force on a system that also carries friction.
- **A 75 kg person in an elevator reads 810 N on a scale. Take up as positive. Find the elevator acceleration.**
  - Answer: 1 m/s²
  - Worked: N − mg = ma, so a = N/m − g. a = 10.8 − 9.8 = 1 m/s², upward.
  - Misconception: Treating the scale reading as the weight.
- **On a frictionless floor, a 25 N push acts on a 3 kg block that touches a 2 kg block. How big is the contact force between them?**
  - Symbolic: C = F m2 / (m1 + m2)
  - Answer: 10 N
  - Worked: System: a = F/(m1 + m2) = 5 m/s². Second block alone: C = m2 a = 10 N.
  - Misconception: Saying the second block feels the full push.
- **A 10 kg box (μs = 0.3) is pushed horizontally with 20 N and does not move. How big is the friction on it?**
  - Answer: 20 N
  - Worked: Not accelerating, so friction balances the push: f = 20 N. μs N = 29.4 N is only the most static friction can supply, not what it supplies now.
  - Misconception: Using f = μs N whenever there is friction.
### Band 4: AP C / F=ma

- **Atwood machine, light pulley and string: 7 kg and 1 kg. Find the tension. First derive T in symbols.**
  - Symbolic: T = 2 m1 m2 g / (m1 + m2)
  - Answer: 17.15 N
  - Worked: Heavier: m1 g − T = m1 a. Lighter: T − m2 g = m2 a. a = (m1 − m2) g / (m1 + m2). Substitute: T = 2 m1 m2 g / (m1 + m2). Limits: m1 = m2 gives T = m g and a = 0. m2 → 0 gives T → 0.
  - Misconception: Setting T = m1 g or m2 g.
- **A string joins 6 kg and 1 kg on a rough floor (μk = 0.25). A 48 N pull on the 6 kg block drags both. Find the tension. Does μ matter?**
  - Symbolic: T = F m2 / (m1 + m2), independent of μ
  - Answer: 6.86 N
  - Worked: System: a = F/(m1 + m2) − μ g. Second block: T − μ m2 g = m2 a, so T = m2 (a + μ g) = F m2/(m1 + m2). μ cancels because friction is proportional to mass on both blocks.
  - Misconception: Assuming more friction raises or lowers the tension.
- **A 3 kg block rides on a 3 kg block (μs = 0.4 between them) on a frictionless floor. The 3 kg block is pulled by a horizontal force. What is the largest force before the top block slips?**
  - Symbolic: Fmax = μs g (m + M)
  - Answer: 23.52 N
  - Worked: The top block is accelerated only by static friction, so amax = μs g. The pair needs F = (m + M) amax. Fmax = 23.52 N.
  - Misconception: Using μs (M g) or μs m g alone.
- **Mass 1 kg hangs from mass 4 kg, which hangs from a cable. The elevator holding the cable accelerates up at 1.5 m/s². Find the tension in the cable. Then find the tension between the masses.**
  - Symbolic: T1 = (m1 + m2)(g + a),  T2 = m2 (g + a)
  - Answer: 56.5 N ; 11.3 N
  - Worked: Whole stack: T1 − (m1 + m2) g = (m1 + m2) a. Lower mass alone: T2 − m2 g = m2 a.
  - Misconception: Giving the same tension to both strings.
### Band 5: Olympiad

- **A uniform 5 kg rope of length 1 m hangs from a 69 N upward pull on its top end. Find the tension in the rope 0.75 m below the top.**
  - Symbolic: T(s) = F (1 − s/L)
  - Answer: 17.25 N
  - Worked: Whole rope: a = (F − Mg)/M = 4 m/s² up. The piece below depth s has mass M(1 − s/L): T = M(1 − s/L)(g + a) = F(1 − s/L). The tension falls linearly to zero at the free end.
  - Misconception: Assuming one tension for the whole rope.
- **Blocks 2 kg, 2 kg, 4 kg in a line on a frictionless floor, joined by light strings. A 60 N pull acts on the 2 kg block. Find the tension between the 2 kg and 4 kg blocks.**
  - Symbolic: T23 = F m3 / (m1 + m2 + m3)
  - Answer: 30 N
  - Worked: System: a = F/(m1 + m2 + m3). The last block alone: T23 = m3 a.
  - Misconception: Using the pull F for every string.
- **A 70 kg person sits in a 5 kg bucket and pulls down on the rope that runs over a ceiling pulley and back to the bucket. They rise at 1.5 m/s². Find the force of the bucket floor on the person.**
  - Symbolic: N = M (g + a) − (M + m)(g + a)/2
  - Answer: 367.25 N
  - Worked: Person plus bucket: two rope segments hold them up, 2T − (M + m) g = (M + m) a. T = 423.8 N. Person: N + T − M g = M a, so N = M(g + a) − T.
  - Misconception: Counting only one rope segment on the system.
- **Three students give the acceleration of a block m1 on a frictionless table tied over a pulley to a hanging m2. Which formula survives the limits "m1 → 0 gives a → g" and "m2 → 0 gives a → 0"?**
  - Answer: a = m2 g / (m1 + m2)
  - Worked: Option 2 blows up as m1 → 0. Option 3 blows up as m2 → 0. Option 4 gives −g when m1 → 0. Only option 1 passes both.
  - Misconception: Trusting a formula that looks like the right shape.
### Band 6: College

- **A force F(t) = 6t newtons pushes a 2 kg block from rest on a frictionless floor. Find the speed at t = 4 s.**
  - Symbolic: v = b t² / (2m)
  - Answer: 24 m/s
  - Worked: a(t) = bt/m. Integrate: v = ∫0^t a dt = b t² / (2m).
  - Misconception: Using v = at with a at the end time.
- **A uniform chain of length 2.5 m lies on a frictionless table with 0.3 m hanging over the edge. Find its acceleration at that instant. Then show that y(t) = y0 cosh(√(g/L) t).**
  - Symbolic: a = g y / L
  - Answer: 1.18 m/s²
  - Worked: Only the hanging part is pulled: (λ y) g = (λ L) a. a = g y/L, so ÿ = (g/L) y, which has cosh solutions.
  - Misconception: Using a = g for a chain that is partly on a table.
- **An Atwood machine with 7 kg and 1 kg hangs from a spring scale. The scale holds a light pulley. What does it read while the masses move?**
  - Symbolic: R = 4 m1 m2 g / (m1 + m2)
  - Answer: 34.3 N
  - Worked: The scale supports the pulley, which feels 2T down. T = 2 m1 m2 g/(m1 + m2), so R = 2T. It is less than (m1 + m2) g because the heavier mass accelerates down.
  - Misconception: Assuming the scale reads the total weight.
- **A 60 N force pulls the far end of a 3 kg uniform rope attached to a 4 kg block on a frictionless floor. Find the force the rope exerts on the block.**
  - Symbolic: T = F M / (M + m_rope)
  - Answer: 34.29 N
  - Worked: System: a = F/(M + m_rope). Block alone: T = M a. A massless rope would give T = F.
  - Misconception: Treating a massive rope as massless.
