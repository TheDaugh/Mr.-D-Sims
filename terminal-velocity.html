Terminal Velocity

Drop a parcel from a helicopter, open the parachute when you choose, and watch the velocity and acceleration graphs.

Setup

Instructions
  1. Press Drop to release the parcel from the helicopter.
  2. Watch the velocity and acceleration graphs. What is happening to the velocity? To the acceleration?
  3. Press Deploy parachute when you want to open it.
  4. Look at both graphs again. How do they compare with before the parachute opened?
  5. Change the Parachute size and drop again. How does it change the rate of descent?
  6. Press Clear & reset to drop a new parcel.
Theory

On the Moon, how fast objects fall does not depend on their size or density, because there is no air. Objects keep accelerating until they hit the surface, which is why the Moon is covered in craters.

On Earth the air resists motion. The resistance grows with the size of the object and with its speed. As a falling object speeds up, the retarding force eventually matches the weight pulling it down. The net force is then zero, the acceleration is zero, and the object falls at constant velocity. That is terminal velocity.

Terminal velocity changes with size. A parachute adds area, so the drag force is bigger at any given speed. When it opens, the parcel slows until the drag has dropped to equal the weight again, at a much lower speed. A bigger parachute means a lower terminal velocity.

The same thing happens on a bicycle freewheeling down a long hill. Crouch down and your terminal velocity rises. Sit upright with your coat open and it falls. Keep your hands on the handlebars.

The model here is ma = mg − kv², where k grows with the area. At terminal velocity v = √(mg/k).
