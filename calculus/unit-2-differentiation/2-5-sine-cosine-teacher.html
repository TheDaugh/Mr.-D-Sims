The four paths (students never see these labels)

1 · Build. Warm-up 0 to 2, or two rough items in a row. Each problem asks for $f'(x)$, then the unit circle value, then $f'(a)$. Hint ladder of three. Simple sums like $3\cos x-2x$ at $\pi/3$.
2 · Apply. Warm-up 3 to 5. Asks for $f'(x)$ and $f'(a)$; the unit circle is on the student. Sine and cosine mixed with powers; limit-in-disguise; tangent line; spring velocity.
3 · Stretch. Warm-up 6/6 clean, or two clean items at Apply. Asks for $f'(a)$ and the tangent line directly. Sign of $f'$ on an interval, solving $f'(x)=0$, limits with a nonzero constant, small-angle estimates.
4 · Extend. Two clean items at Stretch. Result only. The 4-cycle (27th derivative), $f''+f=0$, deriving $\tfrac{d}{dx}\sin x$ from the limit definition with $\sin(x+h)$, spring speed and acceleration.

Rules: two clean items in a row (no misses, no hints) raise the path by one. Two rough ones (three misses, or the worked solution revealed) lower it by one. Middle results hold steady. The preview link lets you open any step and pick a path yourself.

Facilitation notes

The huddle (2 to 3 minutes)

  1. Sketch sine with a few tangent lines: flat at peak and valley, steep on the axis. Ask for the slope graph as a picture before any formula.
  2. Ask: “Cosine starts at a peak. What is its slope at $x=0$? Just after?” (Zero, then negative.) That is where the minus sign comes from.
  3. Ask: “If I shift sine left by $\pi/2$, what do I get?” (Cosine.) So the slope graph of sine is a shifted sine, which is cosine.
  4. Name the rules, then ask someone to say them in words before they type.

Misconceptions to listen for

$\tfrac{d}{dx}\cos x=\sin x$Memorizing “sin and cos swap” and dropping the sign.Ask: is cosine rising or falling just right of 0? A falling curve has a negative slope, and $\sin$ is positive there.
$\tfrac{d}{dx}\sin x=-\cos x$Over-correcting after the cosine rule.Check $x=0$: sine is rising, slope should be $+1$.
Evaluates $f$, not $f'$, at the point.Skips the differentiation step.The tangent is flat at the peak, so the slope there is 0 even though $\sin(\pi/2)=1$.
Degree mode answers.Calculator not in radians.Radian mode, always. The slope of $\sin x$ at 0 is 1 only in radians.
Drops the coefficient, or flips it.$-4\cos x$ becomes $+4\sin x$ wrong by one sign.Two negatives: the $-4$ and the cosine rule’s minus multiply to $+$.

For the student who is not taking the AP exam

Missions 1 and 2 give the concept with no formula: slopes of a wave are read off a picture and the dots draw the answer. The scaffolds and toolbox build the process (differentiate, then read the unit circle). The path moves with accuracy, so a student solid on the picture but shaky on exact values lands in Build or Apply and still finishes with printable notes and a check they can pass.
