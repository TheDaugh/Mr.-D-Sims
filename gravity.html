Measuring g

An electromagnet drops a sphere past two light gates. Measure how long it takes to fall between them, then find g from a graph.

Apparatus

Release the sphere, then store the reading.

#s (m)t (s)t² (s²)
Your value of g

Instructions
  1. Start with the solid sphere. Put the first gate at 0.00 m, right at the top.
  2. Choose where the second gate goes with the Second gate slider.
  3. Press Release. The electromagnet lets go, the sphere cuts the first beam and starts the clock, and the second beam stops it.
  4. Press Store reading to add the fall distance and time to the table.
  5. Press Reset, move the second gate, and repeat. After five or six points the graph of s against t² appears.
  6. Work out g from the line and type it in. Press Check.
  7. Move the first gate down a little and repeat. Then repeat the whole experiment with the hollow sphere. What do you notice?
Theory

The electromagnet holds the sphere just above the first light gate. When the magnet switches off, the sphere falls, blocks the first beam and starts the clock. When it passes the second gate the clock stops.

If the acceleration is uniform, s = ut + ½at². With the first gate right at the top, u = 0 and this becomes s = ½at², where a = g.

A graph of s against t² is a straight line through the origin, and its gradient is exactly ½g. Double the gradient to get g.

Move the first gate down and the sphere is already moving at the first beam, so u is no longer zero. The points stop lying on a line through the origin.

The hollow sphere is light for its size, so air resistance matters. It slows the fall, the slope of the graph flattens, and the gradient no longer represents ½g. The fall heads toward terminal velocity, where the acceleration is zero.
