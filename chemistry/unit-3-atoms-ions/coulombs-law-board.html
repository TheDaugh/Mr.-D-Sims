Why is an electron near the nucleus held harder than one far away?

Shell 1 is 9 times closer than shell 3. Pull × 81.

Vocabulary

Coulomb’s law
The electric force between two charges grows with each charge and shrinks with the square of the distance.
Proportional
Double one quantity, and the other changes by a fixed factor.
Inverse square
Double the distance and the force is ¼. Triple it and the force is 1⁄9.
Electric force
The push or pull between charges. Like charges push apart. Unlike charges pull together.
Nucleus
The positive centre of the atom.
Shell
A region where an electron lives. Shell 2 is 4 times as far out as shell 1.
Nuclear charge
The number of protons, written as + in the sim.

Core ideas

  1. Double the distance, the force is ¼. Triple the distance, it is 1⁄9.
  2. Double one charge, the force is ×2. Double both, it is ×4.
  3. Two changes at once multiply: distance × 2 and one charge × 2 gives ×½.
  4. A near electron and a far electron have the same charge. Only the distance differs.
  5. Shell 1 to shell 3 is 9 times as far, so the pull is divided by 81.
