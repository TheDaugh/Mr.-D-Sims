# Slot 15 Day Card: Effective nuclear charge and shielding (T12, SIM = virtual lab)

Source: UbD Unit 3 Stage 1 to 3 (slot 15). The sim is a **virtual lab**, not a guided lesson: `chemistry/virtual-lab/index.html?lab=zeff` (the Pull Bench). Model: `chemistry/shared/zeff.js`. Lab: `chemistry/virtual-lab/src/ui/zeff.js`, content `content/labs/06-effective-nuclear-charge.json`. Tests: `chemistry/virtual-lab/test/zeff-lab.test.js`, `tests/lessons/zeff-lab.e2e.js`, `tests/labs-display.e2e.js`.

**Big question:** How hard does the nucleus really pull on an outer electron?
**Length:** Mr. D's call. The lab is 20 minutes by default (`lengthMin` in the lab JSON). Add `&min=30` to the address for a longer class; the huddle moves to the middle.
**Shape:** short and exploratory. Free measuring of any atoms (Li to Ca), the Li-Na-K column, predict sodium or magnesium, name a mystery atom, then the paper worksheet.
**One huddle, in the middle (minute 10 of 20): a notes huddle.** Nothing is called at minute 3 or 25. Everyone looks up and copies the board into the seven blanks. Board: `chemistry/whiteboard/index.html#lesson=zeff` (script: `slot-15.board.md`). One code, the day's first, "Huddle code".
**Paper:** `effective-nuclear-charge-lab-worksheet.docx/.pdf` (27 points, printed scales) and its key.

Hidden difficulty: none to manage. Every student sees all eighteen atoms; the mystery atom is seeded by name. The 13 neighbor pairs where the model and measured ionization energy disagree are never asked.
Interpretations I made (in the lab key): pull = Z_eff ÷ n²; Z_eff = Z − core; Li to Ca only; the meter is scaled to the strongest pull in the grid (neon).
