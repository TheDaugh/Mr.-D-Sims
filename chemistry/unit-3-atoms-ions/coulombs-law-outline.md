# Coulomb's Law and the Shell Model (Outline)

**Unit 3, Day 7 (slot 7 of 25). Online, one page. Teacher view: `coulombs-law.html#key`. Projector page: `coulombs-law-board.html`. Scan-and-tweak key: `coulombs-law-key.md`.**
**Target T5 (Core):** I can use Coulomb's law as a proportion (double the distance, quarter the force; double a charge, double the force) to predict how strongly an electron is held. NGSS HS-PS1-1, HS-PS1-2. E3 Part 2 (Coulomb ratios) is scored in slot 12.

## Big idea
Two proportions answer one question. Force follows each charge in a straight line and the distance as 1 ÷ distance². A near electron has the same charge as a far one. Only its distance differs.

## Hidden branching (from the check)
- 2 or more linear guesses, or 3 or fewer right: scaffolded ratio table (division prompts, the guess column hidden) and ladder band 1.
- 7 or 8 right with no linear guesses: no helper columns, a typed guess before every record, a Z_eff look-ahead (slot 15) and two shielding items, ladder band 3.
- Everyone else: standard, ladder band 2.
No route is ever named on the student page. The route and the error counts reach you in the live-view prompt and in the stamp's summary text.

## Misconceptions targeted
- Linear guess (force doubles, or halves, when distance doubles). Part 3.
- Squaring a charge ("double a charge, four times the force"). Part 4.
- Shell n is n times as far as shell 1 (it is n² times). Part 6.
- Far electrons have less charge. The one question.
