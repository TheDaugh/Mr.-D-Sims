Trends Lab

Every atom you measure fills in a tile on the table. Measure enough and the pattern shows up on its own.

  1. One atom. Pick an atom. Its size is measured. Press the button to pull off an outer electron and measure the energy it takes.
  2. One of each. Put two different atoms together. Watch what the electron does, and measure how hard each atom pulls on it.
  3. A bunch. Add as many atoms as you like and see what they make.

Colors show subshells, the regions where an atom's electrons are found. Nothing here is timed except the end of the activity.

During class this opens in full screen. Leaving it is counted, and the teacher has to see you to unlock it.

How to read this

Atoms. Each colored patch is a subshell: blue 1s, teal 2s, pink 2p, lime 3s, orange 3p. The gold block is the nucleus. A bigger atom is drawn bigger, to the same scale for every atom.

Table tiles. A tile stays dark until you measure it. Size is measured when you pick an atom. Ionization energy is measured when you pull an electron off. Electronegativity is measured when two atoms meet. Darker to brighter means smaller to larger.

Electronegativity is how hard an atom pulls on an electron it shares, on a scale of 0 to 4.

Noble gases already have a full outer shell, so they have no electronegativity.
