Instructions
  1. Set the speed, angle and launch height, then press Fire. The dashed line previews the path before you fire.
  2. The green arrow is the horizontal velocity and the orange arrow is the vertical velocity. Watch how each one changes.
  3. Press New target and try to land the ball on the flag. Predict the angle you need before you fire.
  4. Find the angle that gives the longest range from the ground. Then repeat from the top of a 15 m tower.
  5. Turn on air resistance and fire again. Which angle gives the longest range now?
Theory

A projectile is easiest to understand as two separate motions that happen at once.

Horizontal: with no air resistance nothing pushes the ball sideways, so the horizontal velocity v cosθ is constant.

Vertical: gravity pulls down, so the vertical velocity starts at v sinθ and falls by 9.81 m/s every second. The ball slows on the way up, stops at the top, and speeds up on the way down.

The path is a parabola. From the ground with no air, the range is v² sin 2θ / g, which is largest at 45°. Two angles that add up to 90° give the same range. Launch from higher up and the best angle drops below 45°.

Air resistance slows the ball in both directions. The range falls and the path is no longer symmetrical: the descent is steeper than the climb.
