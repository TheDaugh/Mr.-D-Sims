Ticker Timer

A trolley pulls a paper tape through a timer that taps a dot every 0.02 s. Read the spacing of the dots to work out how the trolley moved.

Hover over the tape to measure the gap between two dots.
Experiment

Every dot is 0.02 s after the last. A strip of five gaps is 0.1 s long.

Analyse

Instructions
  1. Pick a trolley motion and press Run the trolley. The tape appears with its dots.
  2. Look at the spacing. Far-apart dots mean the trolley moved fast between taps. Close dots mean slow.
  3. Hover over the tape to measure the gap between two neighbouring dots. Divide by 0.02 s to get the speed at that moment.
  4. Press Cut into strips. Each strip of five gaps covers 0.1 s. The chart stacks the strips side by side, and the line through their tops is a velocity–time graph.
  5. Say how the trolley moved, then work out the acceleration from how fast the strips grow.
  6. Try Mystery tape for a motion you do not know in advance.
Theory

A ticker timer taps a paper tape at a steady rate, here 50 times a second. The tape is fixed to a trolley and pulled through, so each tap leaves a dot. The dots are always 0.02 s apart in time. How far apart they are on the tape depends on how fast the trolley was going.

Wide spacing means high speed and tight spacing means low speed. Equal spacing means constant velocity. Spacing that grows steadily means constant acceleration.

The average velocity across any gap is v = distance / 0.02 s. Cutting the tape every five gaps gives strips that each cover 0.1 s. Their lengths are proportional to the average velocity over that 0.1 s. Standing the strips side by side builds a velocity–time bar chart.

If the strip lengths grow by the same amount each time, the acceleration is constant: a = Δv / Δt = (Δstrip length / 0.1 s) / 0.1 s.
