\section*{4.5 Part 2 $\cdot$ Solving Related Rates: Volumes, Cones and Shadows --- teacher key}
\textbf{Question:} What do I do when the link equation has two changing letters I need to reduce to one?\par
\subsection*{Self-paced lesson}No clock, no trimming, no skipping, no end mark, no stamp. Every tab is required in order; progress saves on the device so a student can stop and finish the next day.\par
\subsection*{Warm-up review (3 easy items)}\begin{itemize}
\item A cone has radius 4 and height 8. When it is filled to depth 6, the surface radius is... \A{3.} The ratio $r/h=4/8=1/2$ never changes, so $r=6/2=3$.
\item $\dfrac{d}{dt}\bigl(h^3\bigr)$ equals... \A{$3h^2\dfrac{dh}{dt}$.} Power rule, then the chain factor.
\item A rate of $-4$ ft\textsuperscript{3}/min for the volume of water in a tank means the water is... \A{draining.} Negative means the volume is shrinking.
\end{itemize}
\subsection*{Independent lesson}No huddle, no teacher talk, no codes. Students read a short picture tutorial (Concept), watch worked problems move by move (I do), then solve generated problems (You do). Add \texttt{\#key} to the lesson address to open every tab with answers marked.\par
\subsection*{Targets}\begin{itemize}\item I can use similar triangles to write one changing letter in terms of another.
\item I can solve a cone or cylinder volume problem for the wanted rate.
\item I can solve a shadow problem with similar triangles.\end{itemize}
\subsection*{Predictions}\begin{itemize}
\item Water is poured into a cone-shaped funnel (point down) at a steady rate. The water level rises... \A{fast at first, then slower and slower.} The cone gets wider at the top, so the same volume has to cover a wider circle and raises the level less each time.
\item A person walks away from a street lamp at a steady speed. Her shadow gets longer at... \A{a steady speed that is slower than hers.} The shadow grows in proportion to how far she is, so its speed is steady. It is slower than hers because the lamp is taller than she is.
\end{itemize}
\section*{Tutorial script, with every question and answer}
\subsection*{Concept tutorial (8 pictures)}
\textit{I can write one changing letter in terms of another with a similar triangle.}\par
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The cone keeps its shape.} As $h$ grows, $r$ grows with it. The ratio $r/h$ never changes: it is $4/8=\dfrac12$. \textit{(Picture: cone. A cone-shaped tank, point down. The blue-green $r$ is the radius of the water surface. The red $h$ is the water depth. Slide $h$.)}\\ \textit{Question:} The water is $h=6$ ft deep. What is the surface radius $r$? \A{Answer: 3 ft.} $r=\dfrac12(6)=3$ ft. The readout under the picture shows the same.
\item \textbf{Two changing letters is too many.} Both $r$ and $h$ change, so $V=\tfrac13\pi r^2h$ would need a product rule. We can make it simpler. \textit{(Picture: cone. The readout also shows the volume $V=\tfrac13\pi r^2h$.)}\\ \textit{Question:} We know $r=\dfrac h2$. Write $V=\tfrac13\pi r^2h$ with only $h$. \A{Answer: $V=\dfrac{\pi}{12}h^3$.} $\tfrac13\pi\left(\dfrac h2\right)^2h=\tfrac13\pi\cdot\dfrac{h^2}{4}\cdot h=\dfrac{\pi}{12}h^3$.
\item \textbf{Now it is one letter.} From $V=\dfrac{\pi}{12}h^3$ we get $\dfrac{dV}{dt}=\dfrac{\pi}{4}h^2\dfrac{dh}{dt}$. \textit{(Picture: cone. With $V$ written in $h$ only, the rate of $V$ needs only the rate of $h$.)}\\ \textit{Question:} With $h=4$ and $\dfrac{dh}{dt}=1$, find $\dfrac{dV}{dt}=\dfrac{\pi}{4}h^2\dfrac{dh}{dt}$. Give it to 2 decimal places. \A{Answer: 12.57 ft\textsuperscript{3}/min.} $4\pi\approx12.57$.
\item \textbf{A relationship vs a moment.} $r=\dfrac h2$ is true at all times. $h=4$ and $r=2$ are true only now. \textit{(Picture: cone. A frozen moment: $h=4$ and $r=2$.)}\\ \textit{Question:} Before differentiating, which may we put into $V=\tfrac13\pi r^2h$? \A{Answer: $r=\dfrac h2$.} A relationship that is always true can go in early. A number for one moment must wait.
\item \textbf{The cylinder needs no help.} In a cylinder the radius is fixed, so there is only one changing letter: $V=\pi R^2h$. \textit{(Picture: cylinder. A cylinder tank. The radius never changes.)}\\ \textit{Question:} For a cylinder tank, we need a similar-triangle step because... \A{Answer: we do not: only $h$ changes.} A fixed radius is a number, so there is no second changing letter to remove.
\item \textbf{A lamp and a shadow.} The big triangle (lamp to the tip of the shadow) and the small triangle (her to the tip) are similar. \textit{(Picture: shadow. A 15 ft lamp. A 6 ft person stands $x$ from it. The red line from the lamp to the ground touches the top of her head. $s$ is the shadow. Slide $x$.)}\\ \textit{Question:} Which proportion matches the two triangles? \A{Answer: $\dfrac{6}{s}=\dfrac{15}{x+s}$.} Height over base is the same for both: the small triangle has base $s$, the big one has base $x+s$.
\item \textbf{Solve for the shadow.} Cross-multiply $\dfrac{6}{s}=\dfrac{15}{x+s}$: $15s=6x+6s$, so $9s=6x$ and $s=\dfrac23x$. \textit{(Picture: shadow. Slide $x$ to 9. Compare the shadow to $x$.)}\\ \textit{Question:} Using $s=\dfrac23x$, find $s$ when $x=9$. \A{Answer: 6 ft.} $\dfrac23(9)=6$ ft. The readout shows the same.
\item \textbf{The rate of the shadow.} Since $s=\dfrac23x$, we get $\dfrac{ds}{dt}=\dfrac23\dfrac{dx}{dt}$. The shadow grows slower than she walks. \textit{(Picture: shadow. The shadow grows in proportion to $x$.)}\\ \textit{Question:} She walks at $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=6$ ft/s. Find $\dfrac{ds}{dt}$. \A{Answer: 4 ft/s.} $\dfrac23(6)=4$ ft/s.
\end{enumerate}
\subsection*{I do: the five moves}\begin{enumerate}\item Draw and name
\item List what you know and want
\item Link with one equation
\item Differentiate in t
\item Plug in last\end{enumerate}
\subsection*{I do (8 steps)}
\textit{I can watch a cone-tank problem and a shadow problem solved in five moves and spot the common slip.}\par
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Name and list.} \textit{Moves 1 and 2 $\cdot$ Name and list.} Water is going in, so $\dfrac{dV}{dt}$ is positive. We want the level's speed $\dfrac{dh}{dt}$. \textit{(Picture: cone. The tank is 8 ft tall with top radius 4 ft. The water has radius $r$ and depth $h$.)}\\ Given: $\dfrac{dV}{dt}=6$ ft\textsuperscript{3}/min\\ Want: $\dfrac{dh}{dt}$ when $h=4$ ft\\ \textit{Question:} Which rate do we WANT? \A{Answer: $\dfrac{dh}{dt}$.} We are asked how fast the level rises, which is the rate of $h$.
\item \textbf{Link, then reduce to one letter.} \textit{Move 3 $\cdot$ Link with one equation.} Start with $V=\tfrac13\pi r^2h$. Use $r=\dfrac h2$ to write it with $h$ only. \textit{(Picture: cone. The ratio $r/h=4/8$ holds for the water at all times.)}\\ Link: $V=\tfrac13\pi r^2h$\\ Similar triangles: $r=\dfrac h2$\\ $V=\dfrac{\pi}{12}h^3$\\ \textit{Question:} Why can we use $r=\dfrac h2$ BEFORE differentiating? \A{Answer: It is true at all times, not just one moment..} The ratio of the tank never changes, so the relationship holds at all times.
\item \textbf{Differentiate.} \textit{Move 4 $\cdot$ Differentiate in t.} Differentiate $V=\dfrac{\pi}{12}h^3$ in $t$. The 3 comes down, and $\dfrac{dh}{dt}$ rides along. \textit{(Picture: chain. Time leads to $h$, and $h$ leads to $V$. The rates multiply.)}\\ $\dfrac{dV}{dt}=\dfrac{\pi}{12}\cdot3h^2\dfrac{dh}{dt}=\dfrac{\pi}{4}h^2\dfrac{dh}{dt}$\\ \textit{Question:} Which is $\dfrac{d}{dt}\left(\dfrac{\pi}{12}h^3\right)$? \A{Answer: $\dfrac{\pi}{4}h^2\dfrac{dh}{dt}$.} $\dfrac{\pi}{12}\times3=\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, and the factor $\dfrac{dh}{dt}$ rides along.
\item \textbf{Plug in last.} \textit{Move 5 $\cdot$ Plug in last.} Put in $\dfrac{dV}{dt}=6$ and $h=4$. Then solve for $\dfrac{dh}{dt}$. \textit{(Picture: cone. The cone at the moment we are asked about: the water is $h=4$ ft deep.)}\\ $6=\dfrac{\pi}{4}(16)\dfrac{dh}{dt}$\\ $6=4\pi\dfrac{dh}{dt}$\\ $\dfrac{dh}{dt}=\dfrac{6}{4\pi}\approx0.48$ ft/min\\ \textit{Question:} Solve $6=4\pi\dfrac{dh}{dt}$. Give it to 2 decimal places. \A{Answer: 0.48 ft/min.} $6/12.57\approx0.48$ ft/min. Positive: the level is rising.
\item \textbf{The common slip.} Slip: putting $r=2$ into $V=\tfrac13\pi r^2h$ first. That makes $r$ a fixed number, so $\dfrac{dr}{dt}$ is lost and the answer is wrong. \textit{(Picture: cone. At this moment $r=2$ and $h=4$, but the tank is still filling.)}\\ Wrong: $V=\tfrac13\pi(2)^2h=\tfrac{4\pi}{3}h$, so $6=\tfrac{4\pi}{3}\dfrac{dh}{dt}$\\ That gives $\dfrac{dh}{dt}\approx1.43$. Too big.\\ \textit{Common slip.}\\ \textit{Question:} Why is 1.43 wrong? \A{Answer: The radius grows as the water rises, but this made it fixed at 2..} The radius changes with $h$. Use the relationship $r=\dfrac h2$ early, and the moment's numbers last.
\item \textbf{Problem 2: a shadow.} \textit{Moves 1 to 3 $\cdot$ Name, list, link.} \textbf{Problem:} A 6 ft person walks away from a 15 ft lamp at 5 ft/s. How fast is her shadow growing? We are given $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=5$ and want $\dfrac{ds}{dt}$, and similar triangles link $x$ and $s$. \textit{(Picture: shadow. A 15 ft lamp. A 6 ft person is $x$ from it. $s$ is her shadow.)}\\ Given: $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=5$ ft/s\\ Want: $\dfrac{ds}{dt}$\\ Link: $\dfrac{6}{s}=\dfrac{15}{x+s}$\\ \textit{Question:} The two triangles are similar. Why? \A{Answer: They share the lamp's angle and each has a right angle..} Two matching angles make triangles similar, so matching sides have equal ratios.
\item \textbf{Solve for s, then differentiate.} \textit{Moves 3 and 4.} Cross-multiply: $15s=6x+6s$, so $s=\dfrac23x$. Then $\dfrac{ds}{dt}=\dfrac23\dfrac{dx}{dt}$. \textit{(Picture: shadow. The shadow is proportional to $x$.)}\\ $15s=6(x+s)$, so $9s=6x$\\ $s=\dfrac23x$\\ $\dfrac{ds}{dt}=\dfrac23\dfrac{dx}{dt}$\\ \textit{Question:} Why is the answer the same at every moment? \A{Answer: $\dfrac{ds}{dt}=\dfrac23\dfrac{dx}{dt}$ has no $x$ or $s$ in it..} There is no moment-dependent letter in the rate, so no plugging in is needed.
\item \textbf{Plug in.} \textit{Move 5 $\cdot$ Plug in last.} Put in $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=5$. \textit{(Picture: shadow. Her speed is 5 ft/s.)}\\ $\dfrac{ds}{dt}=\dfrac23(5)=\dfrac{10}{3}\approx3.33$ ft/s\\ \textit{Question:} Find $\dfrac{ds}{dt}=\dfrac23\cdot5$. Give it to 2 decimal places. \A{Answer: 3.33 ft/s.} $10/3\approx3.33$ ft/s. It is slower than her 5 ft/s.
\end{enumerate}
\subsection*{Big ideas}\begin{itemize}\item Similar triangles give a relationship that is true at all times, like $r=\dfrac h2$. Use it before differentiating.
\item A cylinder has a fixed radius, so only $h$ changes.
\item A number true for one moment (like $h=4$) waits until the last move.
\item For a shadow, the rate is the same at every moment: $\dfrac{ds}{dt}=\dfrac{h}{H-h}\dfrac{dx}{dt}$.\end{itemize}
\section*{Every level, with answers}
\subsection*{Use similar triangles}
\noindent\textbf{Level 1.} A cone-shaped tank (point down) is 8 ft tall and 4 ft in radius at the top. When the water is 4 ft deep, what is the radius of the water surface? Give it to 2 decimal places.\\ \A{Answer: 2 ft} \quad $r=\dfrac{4}{8}(4)=2$ ft.\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Level 2.} A cone-shaped tank (point down) is 8 ft tall and 4 ft in radius at the top. When the water is 7 ft deep, what is the radius of the water surface? Give it to 2 decimal places.\\ \A{Answer: 3.5 ft} \quad $r=\dfrac{4}{8}(7)=3.5$ ft.\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Level 3.} A cone-shaped tank (point down) is 12 ft tall and 6 ft in radius at the top. When the water is 3.6 ft deep, what is the radius of the water surface? Give it to 2 decimal places.\\ \A{Answer: 1.8 ft} \quad $r=\dfrac{6}{12}(3.6)=1.8$ ft.\par\medskip
\subsection*{Write V in one letter}
\noindent\textbf{Level 1.} A cone-shaped tank (point down) is 8 ft tall and 4 ft in radius at the top. Water of depth $h$ has radius $r$ and volume $V=\tfrac13\pi r^2h$. Which line writes $V$ with $h$ only?\\ \A{(A) $V=\dfrac{1\pi}{12}h^3$} \quad (B) $V=\dfrac{3\pi}{4}h^3$ \quad (C) $V=\dfrac{1\pi}{4}h^3$ \quad (D) $V=\dfrac{1\pi}{6}h^3$\\ \A{Answer: A} \quad $r=\dfrac{4}{8}h$. Then $V=\tfrac13\pi\left(\dfrac{4}{8}h\right)^2h=\dfrac{1\pi}{12}h^3$.\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Level 2.} A cone-shaped tank (point down) is 9 ft tall and 3 ft in radius at the top. Water of depth $h$ has radius $r$ and volume $V=\tfrac13\pi r^2h$. Which line writes $V$ with $h$ only?\\ (A) $V=\dfrac{1\pi}{9}h^3$ \quad \A{(B) $V=\dfrac{1\pi}{27}h^3$} \quad (C) $V=\dfrac{1\pi}{3}h^3$ \quad (D) $V=\dfrac{4\pi}{27}h^3$\\ \A{Answer: B} \quad $r=\dfrac{3}{9}h$. Then $V=\tfrac13\pi\left(\dfrac{3}{9}h\right)^2h=\dfrac{1\pi}{27}h^3$.\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Level 3.} A cone-shaped tank (point down) is 4 ft tall and 3 ft in radius at the top. Water of depth $h$ has radius $r$ and volume $V=\tfrac13\pi r^2h$. Which line writes $V$ with $h$ only?\\ (A) $V=\dfrac{1\pi}{4}h^3$ \quad (B) $V=\dfrac{27\pi}{16}h^3$ \quad (C) $V=\dfrac{9\pi}{16}h^3$ \quad \A{(D) $V=\dfrac{3\pi}{16}h^3$}\\ \A{Answer: D} \quad $r=\dfrac{3}{4}h$. Then $V=\tfrac13\pi\left(\dfrac{3}{4}h\right)^2h=\dfrac{3\pi}{16}h^3$.\par\medskip
\subsection*{Solve a volume problem}
\noindent\textbf{Level 1.} Water is pumped into a cylinder tank of radius 3 ft at 18 ft\textsuperscript{3}/min. How fast is the depth rising? Give $\dfrac{dh}{dt}$ to 2 decimal places.\\ \A{Answer: 0.64 ft/min} \quad $18=\pi(3)^2\dfrac{dh}{dt}$, so $\dfrac{dh}{dt}=0.64$ ft/min.\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Level 2.} Water is pumped into a cone-shaped tank (point down, 8 ft tall, top radius 4 ft) at 11 ft\textsuperscript{3}/min. How fast is the depth rising when the water is 7 ft deep? Give $\dfrac{dh}{dt}$ to 2 decimal places.\\ \A{Answer: 0.29 ft/min} \quad $11=\dfrac{16\pi}{64}(7)^2\dfrac{dh}{dt}$, so $\dfrac{dh}{dt}=0.29$ ft/min.\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Level 3.} Water drains from a cone-shaped tank (point down, 12 ft tall, top radius 6 ft) at 10 ft\textsuperscript{3}/min. How fast is the depth changing when the water is 2 ft deep? Give $\dfrac{dh}{dt}$ to 2 decimal places.\\ \A{Answer: -3.18 ft/min} \quad $-10=\dfrac{36\pi}{144}(2)^2\dfrac{dh}{dt}$, so $\dfrac{dh}{dt}=-3.18$ ft/min. Negative: the level falls.\par\medskip
\subsection*{Solve a shadow problem}
\noindent\textbf{Level 1.} A 5 ft person walks away from a 15 ft lamp at 3 ft/s. How fast is the LENGTH of her shadow growing? Give it to 2 decimal places.\\ \A{Answer: 1.5 ft/s} \quad $s=\dfrac{5}{10}x$, so $\dfrac{ds}{dt}=\dfrac{5}{10}(3)=1.5$ ft/s.\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Level 2.} A 4 ft person walks away from a 10 ft lamp at 6 ft/s. How fast is the LENGTH of her shadow growing? Give it to 2 decimal places.\\ \A{Answer: 4 ft/s} \quad $s=\dfrac{4}{6}x$, so $\dfrac{ds}{dt}=\dfrac{4}{6}(6)=4$ ft/s.\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Level 3.} A 9 ft person walks away from a 18 ft lamp at 2 ft/s. How fast is the TIP of her shadow moving along the ground (measured from the lamp)? Give it to 2 decimal places.\\ \A{Answer: 4 ft/s} \quad The tip is at $x+s=\dfrac{18}{9}x$, so its speed is $\dfrac{18}{9}(2)=4$ ft/s.\par\medskip
