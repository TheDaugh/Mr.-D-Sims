\section*{4.5 Part 1 $\cdot$ Solving Related Rates: Ladders and Distances --- teacher key}
\textbf{Question:} How do I solve a related-rates problem when the link is a right triangle?\par
\subsection*{Self-paced lesson}No clock, no trimming, no skipping, no end mark, no stamp. Every tab is required in order; progress saves on the device so a student can stop and finish the next day.\par
\subsection*{Warm-up review (3 easy items)}\begin{itemize}
\item In $x^2+y^2=13^2$, if $x=5$ then $y=$... \A{12.} $y^2=169-25=144$, so $y=12$.
\item $\dfrac{d}{dt}(x^2)$ equals... \A{$2x\dfrac{dx}{dt}$.} Power rule, then the chain factor.
\item A car is driving toward an intersection. Its distance to the intersection has a rate that is... \A{negative.} The distance is getting smaller, so its rate is negative.
\end{itemize}
\subsection*{Independent lesson}No huddle, no teacher talk, no codes. Students read a short picture tutorial (Concept), watch worked problems move by move (I do), then solve generated problems (You do). Add \texttt{\#key} to the lesson address to open every tab with answers marked.\par
\subsection*{Targets}\begin{itemize}\item I can find the missing side of the triangle at the moment.
\item I can solve a ladder problem for the wanted rate.
\item I can solve a two-moving-objects distance problem, with the sign of each rate.\end{itemize}
\subsection*{Predictions}\begin{itemize}
\item A ladder's foot is pulled away from a wall at a steady speed. The top of the ladder slides down... \A{slowly at first, then faster and faster.} When the foot is close to the wall, the ladder is almost straight up, so the top barely moves. Near the end it drops fast.
\item One car drives east at 30 mph and another drives north at 40 mph from the same corner. The distance between them grows at... \A{50 mph.} The distance is the long side of a triangle, so it grows slower than $30+40=70$. The tutorial shows it is 50 mph.
\end{itemize}
\section*{Tutorial script, with every question and answer}
\subsection*{Concept tutorial (7 pictures)}
\textit{I can say how the sides of a changing right triangle depend on each other.}\par
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The sides trade off.} As the foot slides out, the top comes down. The ladder length stays 5 ft. \textit{(Picture: ladder. A 5 ft ladder. $x$ is the foot, $y$ is the top. Slide $x$ and watch $y$. The ladder length never changes.)}\\ \textit{Question:} The foot is $x=4$ ft from the wall on the 5 ft ladder. How high is the top? \A{Answer: 3 ft.} $y^2=25-16=9$, so $y=3$ ft.
\item \textbf{The top barely moves at first.} When $x$ is small, a 1 ft move of the foot drops the top only a little. Near the end the same move drops it far. \textit{(Picture: ladder. Slide $x$ from 1 to 4 and compare how far $y$ drops for each 1 ft of $x$.)}\\ \textit{Question:} With the foot moving at a steady speed, the top falls... \A{Answer: slowly at first and faster later.} For $x$ from 1 to 2, $y$ drops 0.1. From 3 to 4, $y$ drops 1.
\item \textbf{The rates are tied by the link.} Differentiate $x^2+y^2=25$ in time: $2x\dfrac{dx}{dt}+2y\dfrac{dy}{dt}=0$. So $\dfrac{dy}{dt}=-\dfrac{x}{y}\dfrac{dx}{dt}$. \textit{(Picture: ladder. Foot $x=3$, top $y=4$. Red arrows: the foot goes out, the top goes down.)}\\ \textit{Question:} Why does $\dfrac{dy}{dt}$ come out negative when $\dfrac{dx}{dt}$ is positive? \A{Answer: The foot goes out while the top comes down..} The minus sign says $y$ shrinks while $x$ grows.
\item \textbf{Which side is bigger changes the speed.} The ratio $\dfrac{x}{y}$ is the speed ratio. Here $\dfrac43$ means the top falls faster than the foot moves. \textit{(Picture: ladder. Now the foot is $x=4$ and the top is $y=3$.)}\\ \textit{Question:} At $x=4$, $y=3$ with $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=3$ ft/s, find $\dfrac{dy}{dt}=-\dfrac{x}{y}\dfrac{dx}{dt}$. \A{Answer: -4 ft/s.} $-\dfrac43\times3=-4$ ft/s.
\item \textbf{Two cars from one corner.} The two roads meet at a right angle, so $x$, $y$ and $z$ form a right triangle. \textit{(Picture: cars. The blue car goes east. The orange car goes north. The dashed red line $z$ is the distance between them. Slide the time.)}\\ \textit{Question:} Which equation links $x$, $y$ and $z$? \A{Answer: $x^2+y^2=z^2$.} $z$ is the long side of the triangle.
\item \textbf{Both rates add in.} Differentiating gives $2x\dfrac{dx}{dt}+2y\dfrac{dy}{dt}=2z\dfrac{dz}{dt}$. So $\dfrac{dz}{dt}=\dfrac{x\dfrac{dx}{dt}+y\dfrac{dy}{dt}}{z}$. \textit{(Picture: cars. Arrows show both cars moving away from the corner.)}\\ \textit{Question:} At $x=30$, $y=40$, $z=50$ with $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=30$ and $\dfrac{dy}{dt}=40$ mph, find $\dfrac{dz}{dt}$. \A{Answer: 50 mph.} $(900+1600)/50=50$ mph.
\item \textbf{Toward is negative.} If a car drives toward the corner, its distance $x$ shrinks, so $\dfrac{dx}{dt}$ is negative. \textit{(Picture: cars. Imagine the blue car turning around and driving back toward the corner.)}\\ \textit{Question:} A car is 30 mi east of the corner and drives toward it at 20 mph. What is $\dfrac{dx}{dt}$? \A{Answer: $-20$ mph.} Toward means $x$ shrinks, so the rate is $-20$. The 30 is where the car is, not a rate.
\end{enumerate}
\subsection*{I do: the five moves}\begin{enumerate}\item Draw and name
\item List what you know and want
\item Link with one equation
\item Differentiate in t
\item Plug in last\end{enumerate}
\subsection*{I do (7 steps)}
\textit{I can watch a ladder and a two-car problem solved in five moves and spot the common slip.}\par
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Name and list.} \textit{Moves 1 and 2 $\cdot$ Name and list.} The top is falling, so $\dfrac{dy}{dt}$ is negative. We want the foot's rate $\dfrac{dx}{dt}$. \textit{(Picture: ladder. A 10 ft ladder. $x$ is the foot, $y$ is the top.)}\\ Given: $\dfrac{dy}{dt}=-1.5$ ft/s\\ Want: $\dfrac{dx}{dt}$ when $y=6$ ft\\ \textit{Question:} The top slides DOWN at 1.5 ft/s. How is that written? \A{Answer: $\dfrac{dy}{dt}=-1.5$.} Down means $y$ shrinks, so the rate is negative.
\item \textbf{Link and differentiate.} \textit{Moves 3 and 4 $\cdot$ Link and differentiate.} The link is the right triangle. The 10 is fixed, so its rate is 0. \textit{(Picture: ladder. The red arrows show the foot moving out and the top moving down.)}\\ Link: $x^2+y^2=10^2$\\ $2x\dfrac{dx}{dt}+2y\dfrac{dy}{dt}=0$\\ \textit{Question:} Which line is the derivative in time? \A{Answer: $2x\dfrac{dx}{dt}+2y\dfrac{dy}{dt}=0$.} Each square brings a 2 and its own rate. The 10 is fixed, so the right side is 0.
\item \textbf{Find the missing side.} \textit{Move 5 $\cdot$ Plug in last.} We need $x$ at this moment. Put $y=6$ in the link: $x^2+36=100$. \textit{(Picture: ladder. The moment: the top is 6 ft up. The foot distance is missing.)}\\ At $y=6$: $x^2=100-36=64$, so $x=8$\\ \textit{Question:} When $y=6$ on the 10 ft ladder, find $x$. \A{Answer: 8 ft.} $x^2=100-36=64$, so $x=8$ ft.
\item \textbf{Plug in and solve.} \textit{Move 5 $\cdot$ Plug in last.} Put in $x=8$, $y=6$ and $\dfrac{dy}{dt}=-1.5$. Solve for $\dfrac{dx}{dt}$. \textit{(Picture: ladder. All the moment's numbers are in.)}\\ $2(8)\dfrac{dx}{dt}+2(6)(-1.5)=0$\\ $16\dfrac{dx}{dt}=18$\\ $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=1.125$ ft/s\\ \textit{Question:} Solve $16\dfrac{dx}{dt}-18=0$ for $\dfrac{dx}{dt}$. \A{Answer: 1.125 ft/s.} $16\dfrac{dx}{dt}=18$, so $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=1.125$ ft/s. Positive: the foot is moving out.
\item \textbf{Problem 2: two cars.} \textit{Moves 1 to 3 $\cdot$ Name, list, link.} \textbf{Problem:} One car drives east at 30 mph and another drives north at 40 mph from the same corner. How fast is the distance between them growing after 2 hours? Both cars move away from the corner. We want $\dfrac{dz}{dt}$. \textit{(Picture: cars. Two cars leave a corner: blue east, orange north. $z$ is the distance between them.)}\\ Given: $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=30$, $\dfrac{dy}{dt}=40$ mph\\ Want: $\dfrac{dz}{dt}$ at $t=2$ h\\ Link: $x^2+y^2=z^2$\\ \textit{Question:} After 2 hours, how far from the corner is each car? \A{Answer: $x=60$ mi, $y=80$ mi.} Distance is speed times time: $30\times2=60$ and $40\times2=80$.
\item \textbf{Find z, then differentiate.} \textit{Moves 4 and 5.} First find $z$: $60^2+80^2=z^2$. Then differentiate: $x\dfrac{dx}{dt}+y\dfrac{dy}{dt}=z\dfrac{dz}{dt}$. \textit{(Picture: cars. At $t=2$: $x=60$, $y=80$ and $z$ is the long side.)}\\ $z^2=3600+6400=10000$, so $z=100$\\ $60(30)+80(40)=100\dfrac{dz}{dt}$\\ $\dfrac{dz}{dt}=\dfrac{5000}{100}=50$ mph\\ \textit{Question:} Find $z$ when $x=60$ and $y=80$. \A{Answer: 100 mi.} $z^2=10000$, so $z=100$ mi.
\item \textbf{The common slip.} Slip: finding $z$ by adding, $z=60+80=140$. The straight line is the long side of a triangle, so use $x^2+y^2=z^2$. \textit{(Picture: cars. The red dashed line is shorter than the two roads added: $60+80=140$ is not 100.)}\\ Wrong: $z=60+80=140$\\ Right: $z=\sqrt{60^2+80^2}=100$\\ \textit{Common slip.}\\ \textit{Question:} Which line should we use to find $z$? \A{Answer: $z=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$.} $z$ is the long side, so take the square root of the sum of squares.
\end{enumerate}
\subsection*{Big ideas}\begin{itemize}\item The triangle link is $x^2+y^2=z^2$. Differentiating gives $2x\,x'+2y\,y'=2z\,z'$.
\item Find the third side from the link before plugging in the rates.
\item Toward means a negative rate. Away means a positive rate.
\item Ladders: the foot and top rates have opposite signs.\end{itemize}
\section*{Every level, with answers}
\subsection*{Find the missing side}
\noindent\textbf{Level 1.} A 17 ft ladder leans on a wall. The foot is 8 ft from the wall. How high is the top?\\ \A{Answer: 15 ft} \quad $y^2=17^2-8^2=225$, so $y=15$ ft.\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Level 2.} Two cars leave a corner, one east and one north. They are now 6 miles and 8 miles from the corner. How far apart are they?\\ \A{Answer: 10 mi} \quad $z^2=6^2+8^2=100$, so $z=10$ mi.\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Level 3.} A 25 ft ladder leans on a wall. The foot is 7 ft from the wall. How high is the top?\\ \A{Answer: 24 ft} \quad $y^2=25^2-7^2=576$, so $y=24$ ft.\par\medskip
\subsection*{Solve a ladder problem}
\noindent\textbf{Level 1.} A 17 ft ladder slides away from a wall. The foot moves out at 2 ft/s. How fast is the top falling when the foot is 8 ft from the wall and the top is 15 ft up? Give $\dfrac{dy}{dt}$ to 2 decimal places.\\ \A{Answer: -1.07 ft/s} \quad $32+30\dfrac{dy}{dt}=0$, so $\dfrac{dy}{dt}=-32/30=-1.07$ ft/s.\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Level 2.} A 10 ft ladder leans on a wall. The top slides down at 4 ft/s. How fast is the foot moving out when the top is 8 ft up and the foot is 6 ft from the wall? Give $\dfrac{dx}{dt}$ to 2 decimal places.\\ \A{Answer: 5.33 ft/s} \quad $12\dfrac{dx}{dt}-64=0$, so $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=5.33$ ft/s. Positive: the foot is moving out.\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Level 3.} A 10 ft ladder slides away from a wall. The foot moves out at 3 ft/s. How fast is the top falling when the foot is 7 ft from the wall? Give $\dfrac{dy}{dt}$ to 2 decimal places.\\ \A{Answer: -2.94 ft/s} \quad $y=\sqrt{51}\approx7.14$. Then $\dfrac{dy}{dt}=-\dfrac{x}{y}\dfrac{dx}{dt}=-\dfrac{7}{7.14}(3)=-2.94$ ft/s.\par\medskip
\subsection*{Solve a distance problem}
\noindent\textbf{Level 1.} Two cars leave a corner, one east at 40 mph and one north at 40 mph. How fast is the distance between them growing after 3 hours? Give it to 2 decimal places.\\ \A{Answer: 56.57 mph} \quad $x=120$, $y=120$, $z=169.71$. $\dfrac{dz}{dt}=\dfrac{120(40)+120(40)}{169.71}=56.57$ mph.\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Level 2.} A car is 3 miles east of a corner and drives toward it at 60 mph. A second car is 4 miles north of the corner and drives away from it at 54 mph. How fast is the distance between them changing? Give $\dfrac{dz}{dt}$ to 2 decimal places.\\ \A{Answer: 7.2 mph} \quad $5\dfrac{dz}{dt}=3(-60)+4(54)$, so $\dfrac{dz}{dt}=7.2$ mph (the cars are getting farther apart).\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Level 3.} A plane flies level 8 miles up at 200 mph, moving away from a station. When it is 6 miles along the ground, how fast is its distance $s$ from the station growing? Give it to 2 decimal places.\\ \A{Answer: 120 mph} \quad $12(200)=20\dfrac{ds}{dt}$, so $\dfrac{ds}{dt}=120$ mph. It is less than the plane's speed because part of the motion is not along the line to the station.\par\medskip
\subsection*{Toward or away}
\noindent\textbf{Level 1.} A car is driving toward an intersection at 5 mph. Its distance $x$ from the intersection changes at...\\ \A{(A) $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=-5$} \quad (B) $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=5$ \quad (C) $x=5$ \quad (D) $x=-5$\\ \A{Answer: A} \quad The distance $x$ shrinks, so its rate is negative: $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=-5$.\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Level 2.} The foot of a ladder is pulled away from a wall at 2 ft/s. Its distance $x$ from the wall changes at...\\ (A) $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=-2$ \quad (B) $x=2$ \quad \A{(C) $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=2$} \quad (D) $x=-2$\\ \A{Answer: C} \quad The distance $x$ grows, so its rate is positive: $\dfrac{dx}{dt}=2$.\par\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Level 3.} Two ships sail toward each other. The distance $z$ between them shrinks by 9 km each hour. It changes at...\\ (A) $z=9$ \quad (B) $\dfrac{dz}{dt}=9$ \quad \A{(C) $\dfrac{dz}{dt}=-9$} \quad (D) $z=-9$\\ \A{Answer: C} \quad The distance $z$ shrinks, so its rate is negative: $\dfrac{dz}{dt}=-9$.\par\medskip
