Bouncing Graphs

Drop a ball and watch its displacement, velocity and acceleration graphs draw themselves, bounce by bounce.

Setup

Look for

Which graph is a straight line while the ball is in the air? What does its slope tell you? Where is the velocity zero, and where is the ball at that moment?

Instructions
  1. Press Drop and watch all three graphs build up.
  2. Press Pause to stop the ball and read values off the graphs.
  3. Change the drop height and the bounciness and drop again. Which graph changes shape and which only changes scale?
  4. Pick one bounce. Describe the sign of the velocity and the sign of the acceleration at the top, going up, and going down.
Theory

While the ball is in the air only gravity acts on it, so:

  • The acceleration graph is constant, always at −9.81 m s−2.
  • The velocity graph has a constant negative slope, because the velocity changes by the same amount every second.
  • The height graph always curves downward. It is the shape of a parabola, because the height depends on the square of the time.

The velocity alternates between positive (going up) and negative (going down). It passes through zero when the ball is at its maximum height. At that instant the acceleration is still −9.81. The ball is momentarily still and gravity is still pulling.

Each impact flips the velocity from large and negative to smaller and positive in a very short time. That is a very large upward acceleration for a moment, shown by the orange line. Its size depends on how hard the floor is. Between impacts the acceleration returns to −9.81.

Elasticity e here is the ratio of one rebound height to the one before, so the speed after each bounce is √e times the speed before it.
