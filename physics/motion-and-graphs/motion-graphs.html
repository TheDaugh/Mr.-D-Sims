Motion Graphs on a Hill

Drive a car along a hilly road with the throttle and brake, and watch position, velocity and acceleration graphs build as you go.

Driver

Positive is throttle, negative is brake. You can change it while the car is moving.

Look for

With the pedal held steady, what makes the acceleration graph wobble? When the car climbs a hill, which graph shows it slowing down?

Instructions
  1. Press Start. The car begins at 10 m/s at the left end of the road.
  2. Move the Pedal slider while it drives. Use positive values to speed up and negative values to brake.
  3. Watch the three graphs. The black line marks now.
  4. Try this: hold the pedal at zero and let the hills do the work. Match the bumps on the acceleration graph to the hills on the road.
  5. Try to get to the end of the road in under 30 seconds without passing 25 m/s.
Theory

Position, velocity and acceleration are linked by slopes. The slope of the position graph at any moment is the velocity. The slope of the velocity graph is the acceleration.

With a steady pedal on a level road the acceleration graph is flat. On a hill gravity adds its own acceleration along the road, −g sinθ. Uphill it is negative and the car slows, downhill it is positive and the car speeds up. The acceleration graph therefore follows the slope of the road.

The velocity graph is the running total of the acceleration. Where the acceleration is positive the velocity rises, and where it is negative the velocity falls.
