Ballistic Trolley

A trolley fires a ball straight up as it rolls along. Where does the ball come down?

Apparatus

Instructions
  1. Set the trolley speed with the first slider, then press Start trolley. It stops by itself at the far end.
  2. Press Fire ball while it is moving. The ball goes up and lands back in the cannon.
  3. Set the ball's upward speed with the second slider.
  4. Show trajectory plots the path. Steps switches the dots between equal horizontal steps and equal vertical steps.
  5. Fire the ball, then change the trolley speed before it lands. The ball misses.
  6. Challenge: switch to the hollow ball, fire it, and slow the trolley down so the ball still lands in the cannon.
Theory

Projectile motion becomes much easier to understand if it is split into two separate motions.

In the absence of air resistance the horizontal component of the velocity is constant. Here it equals the velocity of the trolley. The vertical velocity starts upward, is continually slowed by gravity until it stops and reverses, and the ball falls back down.

A stationary observer sees the ball follow a parabola. An observer sitting on the trolley sees the ball simply go up and come down, as if the trolley were not moving. It is like a child playing with a ball inside a car travelling along the motorway.

When air resistance becomes significant (at high speed, or when the ball is very light), drag reduces the horizontal velocity of the ball. Imagine throwing a ball straight up through the sun-roof of a fast car: it does not come back to your hands. In this case the ball does not fall back into the cannon.

Challenge: slow the trolley down after launching the hollow ball so you can still catch it.
