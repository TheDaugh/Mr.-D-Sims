Rolling Disc

A disc rolls without slipping. Two points on it have speeds that change as it turns. When are they equal?

The problem

A disc rolls without slipping. Point A starts at the top of the rim. Point B starts halfway between the centre and A. Find when A's speed first matches B's, using the rule chosen below.

Step 1 · Predict

Instructions
  1. Choose Problem 7 numbers or Problem 8 numbers, or set your own radius and speed. The comparison rule is chosen for you with the buttons.
  2. Work out the time, the speed at that moment and the distance on paper. Enter all three.
  3. Press Roll & check. The disc rolls through one turn. The two traced curves are the paths of A and B. The vertical line on the graph marks the first time the speeds match.
  4. Look at the dashed lines from the contact point to each marked point. A point's speed is its distance from the contact point multiplied by the angular speed.
Theory

A rolling disc without slipping can be seen two ways. In the first, the centre moves forward at v while the disc spins about the centre at ω = v/R. A point on the disc has the centre's velocity plus its spin velocity. At the top, the two add and the speed is 2v. At the bottom they cancel and the speed is zero.

In the second, the whole disc is turning about the point touching the ground, the instantaneous axis. Every point's speed is then ω times its distance from the contact point. This makes each speed easy to write.

A point at distance ρ from the centre and turned φ from the top has a speed v√(1 + (ρ/R)² + 2(ρ/R)cosφ). For a rim point this simplifies to 2v cos(φ/2). The turn angle is tied to the distance moved: φ = (distance)/R.

Setting A's speed equal to B's gives a single equation for φ, and then t = φR/v.
