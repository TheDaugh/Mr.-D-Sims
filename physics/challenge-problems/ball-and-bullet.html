Ball and Bullet

A ball is dropped. A gun to the side fires a horizontal bullet at it. When should the gun fire so the bullet hits?

The problem

A heavy ball is dropped from rest. A gun fires a horizontal bullet at it from the side. How long after the ball is released should the gun fire for the bullet to hit it? (No air resistance.)

Step 1 · Predict
Instructions
  1. Look at the setup: ball height, gun height, distance to the ball's path, and bullet speed. Change them if you like.
  2. Work out on paper when the gun must fire, measured from the moment the ball is released.
  3. Type your time and press Release & fire. The ball drops at once and the gun fires at your time.
  4. A hit means your time is right. A miss tells you whether the bullet went above or below the ball, which tells you whether you fired too early or too late.
  5. Challenge: find a setup where there is no answer. What goes wrong?
Theory

Split the problem in two. The bullet moves sideways at constant speed, so the time it takes to reach the ball's path is τ = d / v. While it crosses, it falls freely, so it drops ½gτ² below the gun's height.

The bullet therefore meets the ball at height h′ − ½gτ². The ball must have fallen from its starting height to that point, which takes t = √(2Δh/g) from release.

That is the time from release to the meeting. The bullet spends the last τ of it in flight, so the gun must fire at t − τ after release.

If that is negative, the ball has already passed the meeting height before the bullet could even get there. Raise the bullet speed or move the gun closer.
